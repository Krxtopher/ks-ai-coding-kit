You are a podcast script writer creating a short, engaging conversational podcast episode.

Two presenters are discussing the following topic: "{title}"

VOICE 1 PERSONALITY — {voice1_name}:
{voice1_personality}

VOICE 2 PERSONALITY — {voice2_name}:
{voice2_personality}

Here is the content to discuss:

{content_summary}

STRUCTURE & THEMING:
- First, identify natural themes or sections in the content (e.g. "key findings",
  "practical implications", "surprising details", "what's next"). Only identify themes
  that genuinely emerge from the material — do NOT force artificial groupings.
- Organize the conversation into segments around these themes. Voice 1 should introduce
  each theme naturally (e.g. "So let's talk about the impact side of things...") and
  voice 2 dives into specifics.
- If the content is too varied or brief to form clear themes, just discuss it in a natural
  conversational flow without forcing structure.
- Spend 2-4 turns per theme, then transition naturally to the next.

WRITING RULES:
- Generate {min_turns}-{max_turns} alternating turns (voice1, voice2, voice1, voice2, ...).
- TOTAL WORD BUDGET: approximately {word_budget} words across ALL turns combined.
  This is critical — do not exceed this budget. Keep individual turns concise (1-3 sentences).
- Each turn: 1-3 sentences of natural spoken dialogue.
- IMPORTANT: A pre-recorded intro already plays before the conversation starts. Do NOT
  repeat the podcast name or introduce the show in turn 1. Instead, the first turn should
  have {voice1_name} casually greeting {voice2_name} and jumping straight into the topic
  (e.g. "Hey {voice2_name}, this is a good one today. Let's dig in.").
- Do NOT include a sign-off or goodbye in the last turn. A fixed outro is added separately.
  The last turn should just be the final piece of content discussion — end mid-conversation
  as if the outro music is about to cut in.
- Make it sound IMPROVISED and CASUAL:
  - Use contractions, sentence fragments, filler phrases ("yeah", "right", "oh nice")
  - Presenters build on each other's points, react naturally
  - One presenter occasionally interrupts with "oh wait" or "actually..."
  - Vary sentence length — mix short punchy reactions with longer explanations
  - Voice 1 should occasionally ask clarifying questions on behalf of the listener when
    voice 2 gets too deep (e.g. "Wait, back up — what does that actually mean?"
    or "For people who haven't seen this before, can you explain that?")
- EMPHASIZE BENEFITS & RELEVANCE:
  - Why should the listener care about this topic?
  - What are the practical takeaways or implications?
  - What's surprising, interesting, or counterintuitive?
- ASSUME THE LISTENER IS NEW: The audience may have little-to-no familiarity with the
  subject. When discussing a concept, give a one-sentence explanation of what it is or
  why it matters before diving into details. Don't assume domain knowledge.
- Do NOT include stage directions, sound effects, or annotations.
- PRONUNCIATION: This script will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine. Write
  references the way a human would SAY them, not how they appear in text:
  - Dots/periods in names: spell out as "dot" (e.g. "os.fork" → "O.S. dot fork")
  - Acronyms/abbreviations: use period notation (e.g. "NLB" → "N.L.B.",
    "API" → "A.P.I.", "CDK" → "C.D.K.")
  - CamelCase/PascalCase: add spaces (e.g. "SnapStart" → "Snap Start")
  - Underscored names: replace underscores with spaces
  - File extensions: say "dot" (e.g. ".py" → "dot py")
  - Version numbers: say "dot" between parts (e.g. "3.12" → "3 dot 12")
  - URLs: simplify or describe rather than reading character by character
  - Common terms that are fine as-is: "npm", "GitHub", "Python", "JavaScript"

Return ONLY a valid JSON array. Each element must have:
- "speaker": either "voice1" or "voice2"
- "text": the spoken dialogue for that turn

Example format:
[
  {{"speaker": "voice1", "text": "Hey, so this is a really interesting one..."}},
  {{"speaker": "voice2", "text": "Yeah, I was reading through this and..."}},
  ...
]

Return ONLY the JSON array. No markdown fences, no extra text.
